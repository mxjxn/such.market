# such.market: a manifesto

## much grow. very forever.
We were born into a machine that only knows one direction. Every quarter has to beat the last, every city gets more expensive, every year costs more than the one before. Rent climbs faster than pay. Two incomes do what one used to. Our time gets cut into billable pieces, our hobbies turn into side hustles, and even rest has to be earned back from a system that never stops. Nobody voted for it. It's just the water we were dropped into.

## such toll. many gate.
To trade, save, or sell, you go through someone who keeps a cut. Those roads were paved centuries ago by people who understood that whoever owns the road owns the traffic. The names and logos change, but the shape stays the same: value flows up, and the rest of us pay rent on our own exchanges. The squeeze isn't a malfunction. It's the design.

## wow. but what if no.
Then a strange new tool showed up: a way for two strangers to make an agreement that holds with no one in the middle. A ledger nobody owns. A promise that keeps itself.

A lot of what grew around it turned into speculation and casino tables. Some of that is fun, some of it funds real builders, and we won't pretend we're above it. But it was never the main point. Underneath, the tool still does what it did on day one. It lets people deal with each other directly.

## very glass. no hide.
Here's the part people tend to skip: this tool is not private. Every trade is public, permanent, and tied to a wallet anyone can read. It's bad at secrets and good at doing business in the open, where your word becomes your record and nobody can quietly rewrite the terms.

That doesn't mean everyone has to stand fully exposed. A provider can offer goods or services under a shared, vetted name, and the trade stays public while the person behind it stays shielded. That's not full anonymity, and we won't pretend it is. It's honest business in public, with room to breathe.

## very human. much old.
People traded what they had for what they needed long before clearinghouses existed. The farmer and the carpenter. The neighbor with eggs and the neighbor with an afternoon to spare. It faded because you couldn't trust strangers, carry your credit across town, or prove what you were owed. Those are exactly the problems this tool solves.

## so build.
such.market is a small road beside the old ones, not a war on them. Offer what you have for what you want, any bundle for any bundle, out in the open. Unknown artists don't need permission to show up. The one small fee is written where anyone can read it. Junk gets filtered, dead offers get cleared, and you decide what you see. It starts with art and tokens, because that's where these tools already live. It won't end there.

**The machine says grow or die. We say trade, and live.**
